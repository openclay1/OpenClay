# Exhibit A: GLP-1 Receptor Agonist Literature Review

## Executive Summary

Three landmark phase 3 trials — STEP 1, SURMOUNT-1, and SELECT — demonstrate that GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide) produce clinically significant weight reduction (14.9–20.9%) and, in the case of semaglutide, a 20% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events in adults with overweight or obesity. However, all three trials enrolled predominantly white cohorts (67–75%), excluded or underrepresented patients with type 2 diabetes, and did not assess long-term durability of weight loss after discontinuation. Cost-effectiveness data remain absent. These gaps directly limit the applicability of current evidence to Hispanic/Latino populations, including Puerto Rico, where obesity prevalence exceeds 36% and cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death.

## Gap Analysis

- **Mechanism:** How GLP-1 agonists reduce cardiovascular events beyond weight loss is not fully understood — anti-inflammatory, anti-atherosclerotic, and direct cardiac pathways remain hypothetical.
- **Diversity:** All three trials had low representation of Hispanic/Latino, Black, and Indigenous participants. Findings cannot be assumed to generalize.
- **Durability:** No trial studied weight regain after treatment cessation. Clinicians lack data on maintenance protocols.
- **Economics:** No cost-effectiveness analysis was conducted, a critical omission for health systems with constrained budgets.

## Local Relevance — Puerto Rico

Puerto Rico has one of the highest obesity rates in U.S. jurisdictions (36.4%, CDC 2024) and disproportionate cardiovascular mortality. The island's population is almost entirely Hispanic/Latino — the demographic most underrepresented in every trial reviewed. GLP-1 agonists are increasingly prescribed on the island, yet the evidence base does not reflect Puerto Rican patients' metabolic profiles, dietary patterns, or pharmacogenomic context. Local investigators are uniquely positioned to fill this gap with community-based studies that reflect the population actually receiving these medications.
