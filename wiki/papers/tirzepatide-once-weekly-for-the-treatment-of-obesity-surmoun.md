## Key Findings
Mean weight reduction at 72 weeks was −15.0% (5 mg), −19.5% (10 mg), and −20.9% (15 mg) vs −3.1% with placebo (all p<0.001). 85-91% of tirzepatide participants achieved ≥5% weight loss. Improvements in all prespecified cardiometabolic measures including waist circumference, lipids, and fasting insulin. The dual incretin mechanism (GIP + GLP-1) may provide additive metabolic benefits versus GLP-1 alone.

## Methodology
Phase 3, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial. 2539 adults with BMI ≥30 (or ≥27 with at least one weight-related comorbidity) received tirzepatide (5, 10, or 15 mg) or placebo subcutaneously once weekly for 72 weeks. Dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist mechanism.

## Limitations
Predominantly white population (67.6%). Cardiovascular outcomes not directly measured — inferred from surrogate markers. Durability of weight loss after treatment cessation unknown. Comparison with semaglutide was not a study objective — no head-to-head data. GI adverse events (nausea, diarrhea, constipation) remain dose-limiting for some patients.
