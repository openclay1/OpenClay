## Key Findings
Semaglutide reduced MACE by 20% versus placebo (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72–0.90, p<0.001). All-cause mortality showed a nonsignificant trend favoring semaglutide (HR 0.81, p=0.052). Mean weight loss was 9.4% with semaglutide vs 0.9% with placebo. Benefits were consistent across subgroups regardless of baseline BMI, age, sex, and race. Confirmed that cardiovascular benefit extends beyond glucose-lowering effects of GLP-1 receptor agonists.

## Methodology
Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled cardiovascular outcomes trial. 17,604 adults aged ≥45 with BMI ≥27, established cardiovascular disease, and no diabetes. Subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo once weekly. Mean follow-up 39.8 months. Primary endpoint: composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke (MACE).

## Limitations
Excluded patients with type 2 diabetes — findings may not extend to diabetic populations where cardiovascular risk mechanisms differ. Relatively low representation of non-white participants. Mechanism by which semaglutide reduces MACE is not fully elucidated — may involve anti-inflammatory, anti-atherosclerotic, or direct cardiac effects beyond weight loss. Long-term safety beyond 5 years unknown. All-cause mortality did not reach statistical significance.
